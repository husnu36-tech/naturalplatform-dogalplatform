### **1\. Assimilation and the Dictate of Silence in the Name of "Integration"**

One of the most frequently used terms regarding foreigners in Germany is **"integration."** In practice, however, this term carries a very telling hidden meaning: **"Keep your mouth shut, don't stand out, conform, and contribute to the smooth functioning of the system."**

As long as you produce economic value for the system and obey without question, you are tolerated. But the moment you demand your rights, expose injustices, or remind them of your constitutional rights, the answer—implicit or explicit—is always the same: *"Our rules are what they are; if you don't like it, you can leave."*

This approach cannot be explained by the universal principles of social democracy, but solely by a policy of conditional acceptance.

### **2\. Bureaucratic Reason and Institutional Coldness**

The most defining characteristic of the German state apparatus is "rational-bureaucratic domination," as described by Max Weber a century ago. In this structure, human empathy, personal conscience, or flexibility are viewed as "system errors."

* With the police, local administrative offices (*Ordnungsamt*), doctors, the domestic intelligence service (*Verfassungsschutz*), and social workers, all institutions act as if they are controlled from a single center—with a perfect, cold synchronization.  
* When foreigners get caught up in this machinery, the institutions do not see the human being as an individual, but merely as a "file" or a "case" to be managed or neutralized.  
* The fact that severe violations of the General Equal Treatment Act (*AGG*)—such as the prohibition to speak one's mother tongue (Turkish) at the workplace—mobbing, and economic pressure are completely ignored by institutions proves how protective and discriminatory this machinery truly is.

### **3\. "Whatever Happened to the Working Class?" – The Failure of Solidarity Networks**

One of the most painful experiences in this process is the fact that structures that theoretically preach "universal working-class solidarity" and anti-fascism (unions, leftist parties, migrant organizations, and initiatives) are entirely dysfunctional in practice.

When a person going from door to door to share the injustices they have suffered, petitioning parliaments, is turned away by these organizations using bureaucratic excuses like "3 months of membership required," it exposes the deep gap between theoretical rhetoric and practical reality. When things get heated, these structures duck away and become mere guardians of the status quo.

### **4\. What is the System Afraid Of?**

The fact that all these mechanisms are set in motion with such force against a single individual has only one reason: **Fear.**

The system deals exceptionally well with people who cower, live in fear, and retreat into their corner. But when someone emerges who knows their rights, files criminal charges with the police, goes to court, and refuses to stay silent, panic breaks out. Suspensions, salary cuts, the cancellation of transit tickets, and intimidation attempts are, in truth, merely an expression of the system's own fragility and its panic in the face of the justice of this resistance.

### **5\. Time for Work, Bread, and Freedom: The Struggle Against Fascism and Remilitarization**

What is happening here is not an isolated individual case; it is a symptom of the escalating imperialist policies, militarism, and xenophobia worldwide and in Germany. Against these wars, remilitarization, and the crushing of peoples, we must unite—true to the spirit of the **Popular Front**, as we see it in France and all around the world.

* We must defend the social state, save it from being a mere paper lie, and fundamentally develop it further.  
* **"Work, Bread, and Freedom"** is the most fundamental human demand. Around this demand, native-born and foreign-born workers must stand shoulder to shoulder.

### **Conclusion and Call to Action**

This article is not just the chronicle of an individual ordeal, but a critical mirror of the attitude that German institutions adopt toward foreigners and those seeking justice.

The true degree of justice in a state or society is measured by how it treats the weakest, the disadvantaged, and those who have the courage to question the system. If "social democracy" is a privilege that applies only to the "conforming citizen," then the word "social" in its name is nothing more than a hollow phrase.

As long as there are people who do not remain silent, who do not bow down, and who bring the truth to light, the struggle for justice and freedom will go on.
