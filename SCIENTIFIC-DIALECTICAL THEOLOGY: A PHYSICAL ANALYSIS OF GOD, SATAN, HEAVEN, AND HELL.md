The Limits of Science: Why Is There No Definitive Answer?

The scientific method relies on the principle of **falsifiability**. For a hypothesis to be considered scientific, it must be testable through experimentation and capable of being proven false. Because concepts like God or the afterlife are defined beyond time and space, they remain outside the scope of scientific measurement tools (telescopes, particle accelerators, etc.).

While renowned physicist Stephen Hawking argued that the universe can create itself out of nothing, physicist Georges Lemaître (one of the pioneers of the Big Bang theory) argued that science does not contradict the existence of God.

In summary; while science answers the question "How?" (how the universe works, how the brain perceives), religion and philosophy focus on "Why?" Therefore, the subject remains a matter of philosophical preference and faith rather than a scientific formula.
